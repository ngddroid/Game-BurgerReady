## 0. One-paragraph summary

A 3D isometric **arcade-idle burger tycoon** for Android. You play an animal chef who grills, stacks and carries burgers, serves animal customers, collects cash and spends it on build pads. It plays like the reference genre, but it's split into **10,000 handcrafted-by-script levels (shifts)**. Levels 1–10 are tutorials you can't lose. After level 10, difficulty rises along a tested curve with relief levels, and every 10th level is a boss. Between levels you spend Bucks on permanent upgrades, open **Stores** around the world (each one earns passive income and has its own look), and buy **Themes, Chef Skins and Pets**. It's built natively in **Kotlin**: a custom, lightweight **OpenGL ES 3 toon renderer** that targets 60 fps on your Realme, with **Jetpack Compose** for all menus. Every 3D model, render, icon, sound and the music are **generated by our own scripts** (Blender + Python), so no third-party art is used and nothing is copied from the reference app.

**Player pain points in reference reviews:** ads too often ("every 10 seconds"), billboard ads still showing after paying to remove ads, lag and freezes, boosters lost on restart, staff carrying only 1 item, progress walls between shops, and a confusing theme switch (a pizza game that turns into a burger shop).

### 1.4 Opportunities we exploit
1. A **level-based structure** (10,000 short shifts with stars). Neither Supercent game has one, and it gives clear goals and good session length.

3. **Respectful ads** later: no billboard ads, no interstitial spam, boosters that persist.
4. **Smooth on low-end phones** (lag is a top complaint). Your Realme is exactly that class of device.
5. **No progress walls:** Stores pay passive income, and failed levels still pay a consolation reward.

## 2. "No violation" guardrails

### 2.1 Name and identity
- **Don't use "Burger Ready".** It is already used by two Play apps, and it echoes the reference's "Pizza Ready!" name. That risks Play's **Impersonation** policy ("misleading users about an app's connection to another app") and trademark complaints.
- **Recommended: "Bun Voyage!"** with the subtitle *Burger Tycoon*. It fits the travel-the-world Stores theme, and I found no existing app with that name. Alternative: **"Patty Paws"** (also no match found). You choose; it's a one-line change.
- The package id `com.ngstudio.burgerready` can stay, because users don't see it. Only the app label, icon and store listing change.

### 2.2 Originality rules I will follow
- **Zero** files from the reference APK. All 3D models, textures, icons, UI art, sound effects and music are generated by our scripts in `tools/`.
- A different **art direction**: animal-folk characters, a warm "sunset diner" palette, wood/terrazzo floors, round plate-shaped pads, gold-coin cash, cartoon outlines, day→night lighting per shift. The reference uses faceless blue/red humans, beige+pink tiles, orange diamond pads and green bills.
- Different **structure**: levels, stars, bosses, troubles, mutators, Stores with passive income, pets.
- No competitor names in the code, UI, store text or keywords. No real brands, logos or restaurant chains.
- Fonts are OFL-licensed (Google Fonts). All other content is ours.

### 2.3 Play policy checklist (built in from day 1)
- Target audience **13+** (it avoids Families-policy ad restrictions later). Content rating will be "Everyone".
- Privacy Policy and Terms bundled in the app (HTML in assets) **and** a hosted URL for the Play listing. You will need to host the same HTML on any free page.
- No data collected in v1 (no ads or analytics yet), so the Data Safety form is simple. When ads arrive: Google UMP consent, AD_ID permission, updated Data Safety.
- **Release builds contain no fake or mock ad UI.** The "Watch Ad" dialog exists only in debug builds (see §6.6).
- Target SDK 37 (already set). No native libraries of our own, so 16 KB page-size compliance is automatic. Release builds are R8-minified.

## 3. Game concept

### 3.1 Pitch
*Grill it, stack it, serve it, then take your burger empire on a world tour.* Chef **Patty the Corgi** starts with a tiny food truck and builds burger joints from a seaside shack to a neon Tokyo alley to a space-station diner, serving a crowd of hungry animal customers across 10,000 shifts.

### 3.2 Pillars
1. **Juicy hands-on arcade.** Every action pops: squash and stretch, particles, rising-pitch sounds, haptics.
2. **Readable goals.** Each level is a 2–4 minute shift with 1–3 star goals.
3. **Always progressing.** Upgrades, Stores, skins, pets and collections keep moving even when a level is hard.
4. **Runs great on cheap phones.** 60 fps on a Mali-G57 MP1.

## 4. In-level gameplay (one shift)

### 4.1 Controls
- Floating joystick anywhere on the lower 70 % of the screen, with a dead-zone and an analog speed curve.
- Everything else happens by **standing in a zone**: pick up, drop, pay, cashier, clean, fix. No tapping is needed during play except the HUD buttons.

### 4.2 Core loop
1. **Stations produce** items onto output trays (grill → burgers, fryer → fries, fountain → sodas, blender → shakes).
2. **You carry** a swaying spring-physics stack. Capacity starts at 4 and grows with upgrades. Mixed stacks are allowed.
3. **You drop** items on the counter's display racks (one rack per product).
4. **Customers queue**, show an order bubble (for example 🍔×2 🍟×1) and are served when the counter has their items and a cashier (you or staff) is at the register.
5. **They pay** gold coins, which pile up. Walk over the pile to collect.
6. **Dine-in** guests sit, eat (with a chomp animation) and leave a tray. Carry trays to the trash bin to free the table.
7. **Drive-thru** cars queue in their own lane, pay more, and are served from the window rack.
8. **Spend cash on pads**: build stations, add tables, **hire staff** (Cashier, Runner, Cleaner), upgrade stations (Lv1→3), expand walls.

### 4.3 Extra mechanics (unique to us)
| Mechanic | Details |
|---|---|
| **Combo meter** | Serving a customer within 40 % of their patience raises the combo from ×1.0 to ×3.0 tips. A walkout resets it. The HUD flame grows with the combo |
| **Rush Waves** | Siren banner and the music layer intensifies; 8–20 s of 2–3× spawn rate |
| **Power-ups on the floor** | ☕ Espresso (speed), 🧲 Magnet (coin pull radius), ✨ Golden Spatula (instant cook 10 s). These are plain pickups, not ads |
| **Troubles** | Ketchup spill (slows you until wiped), grease fire (stand near it to extinguish), raccoon thief (chase it to get the burger back), jammed machine (stand 2 s to fix), sleepy guest (poke awake). An off-screen arrow points to each |
| **VIP guest** | Gold crown, ×3 pay, short patience |
| **Food Critic** | Boss levels. A rating bar must stay high |
| **Day→night** | Visual and mood only, except in Night Shift mutator levels |

### 4.4 Level end
- **Win:** the primary goal is met before the timer ends (or the moment it's met, if "early finish" is on). The results screen then shows stars, Bucks, a gem chance, chest progress and Recipe-card drops.
- **Fail (only levels 11+):** the timer runs out first. You get a consolation reward of 25 % of the Bucks, then Retry or Home. *Later with ads: "+30 s" revive.*
- **Levels 1–10 cannot fail.** The timer turns into "Overtime" and the shift continues until the goal is met.

## 5. 10,000 levels and the difficulty script

### 5.1 Structure
- **Chapter** = 10 levels (1,000 chapters). Slot 10 of each chapter is a **Boss Level**.
- Each level is produced by `LevelGenerator.generate(levelId)`. It is **deterministic**: the same level always plays the same, so nothing large has to be stored. The seed is a hash of levelId.
- Ingredients per level: layout template, mirroring, station placement, product set, customer mix, spawn curve, rush waves, troubles, mutators, unlock pads, goals, star thresholds and reward.
- **12 layout templates** are introduced progressively: Food-Truck Stand, Corner Grill, Classic Diner, Diner + Patio, Drive-Thru Diner, L-Bistro, Twin Counters, Food Court, Boardwalk, Two-Room, Mega Diner, Burger Palace. Each is mirrored and its station slots are permuted by seed, giving 200+ distinct arrangements. They're rendered in the look of whichever **Store** you have selected.

### 5.2 Levels 1–10: handcrafted tutorial (always winnable)
| # | Name | Teaches | Goal | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Grand Opening | Move, build grill ($10) and counter ($20), carry, serve, collect | Earn $60 | Arrows + hand pointer, no timer |
| 2 | Stack 'Em High | Bigger stacks, second grill | $100 | Timer shown, Overtime on |
| 3 | Take a Seat | Tables, cleaning trays | $150 | — |
| 4 | Fry Time | Fryer, multi-item orders | $220 | — |
| 5 | First Rush! | Rush wave + combo meter | $300 | — |
| 6 | Fizz Fountain | Soda station | $360 | — |
| 7 | Hire a Helper | Hire Cashier pad | $450 | — |
| 8 | VIP Visit | VIP guest | $520 | — |
| 9 | Drive-Thru Debut | Drive-thru lane + cars | $600 | — |
| 10 | Critic's Day (boss) | Critic rating bar | $700 | Celebration; unlocks Stores tab, Daily Spin, Recipe Book |

### 5.3 Difficulty curve (levels 11–10,000)
Let `t = n − 10`.

```
D_base(n) = 0.15
          + 0.35 · (1 − e^(−t/60))      // fast learning ramp, mostly done by L250
          + 0.40 · (1 − e^(−t/900))     // long mastery climb, mostly done by L3000
          + 0.10 · (t / 9990)           // gentle creep to the very end

Chapter sawtooth by slot s = 1..9:
  mod = [−0.05, −0.03, −0.01, 0.00, +0.01, +0.02, +0.03, +0.04, +0.06]
Every 50th level is a "breather": mod −= 0.08
Seeded micro-jitter: ±0.01
D(n) = clamp(D_base + mod, 0, 1)
Boss (s = 10): D_base + 0.05 and special rules (§5.5)
```

**Sample values:** L11 ≈ 0.15 · L50 ≈ 0.37 · L100 ≈ 0.46 · L250 ≈ 0.59 · L500 ≈ 0.67 · L1000 ≈ 0.78 · L2500 ≈ 0.90 · L5000 ≈ 0.95 · L10000 = 1.0.
Within every chapter the difficulty climbs from slot 1 to slot 9, then the boss, then it drops back for slot 1 of the next chapter. This is the sawtooth that keeps players from burning out.

### 5.4 What D controls
| Parameter | D = 0 | D = 1 |
|---|---|---|
| Customer spawn interval | 5.5 s | 1.4 s |
| Patience | 60 s | 18 s |
| Average items per order | 1.0 | 3.6 |
| **Required efficiency** (target ÷ bot's theoretical max revenue) | 0.40 | 0.86 |
| Troubles per minute | 0 | 2.5 |
| Mutators | 0 (D < 0.35) | 1 (0.35–0.7), 2 (> 0.7) |
| Shift length | 150–240 s (by layout size) | same |
| Starting cash / pad count | generous | tight |

**Feature schedule (introduced once, then mixed in):** L12 cheese burger · L15 spills · L18 Runner staff · L25 double stack + grease fire · L30 Cleaner staff · L35 raccoon thief · L40 milkshake · L50 mutators begin · L60 veggie · L75 jammed machine · L100 combo meals · L150+ two-room layouts · L200+ everything is in the pool, and difficulty comes from D plus mutators.

**Mutators (L50+):** Hungry Hour, Picky Eaters, Tip Frenzy, Slippery Floors, Critic Inspection, Night Shift, Heat Wave (drinks ×2), Big Party (groups), Double Drive-Thru.

### 5.5 Boss rotation (every 10th level)
Critic Day → Festival Rush → Celebrity Party (one giant order) → Eating Contest (supply X in 60 s) → Food-Truck Rally (deliver to trucks) → Grand Reopening (build-from-scratch speedrun) → repeat. Bosses pay ×2 Bucks plus Gems.

### 5.6 Stars
- 1★ = primary goal met.
- 2★ = goal ×1.2, **or** finished with 25 % of the time left.
- 3★ = goal ×1.45 **and** zero walkouts.
- The next level unlocks when the current one has **≥ 1★**.

### 5.7 Proof that all 10,000 levels are "perfect": the Level Validator
A unit test, `AllLevelsValidatorTest`, runs on the JVM and goes through **every level 1–10,000**:
1. It generates the level and runs the **headless game simulation** with a scripted **Expert Bot** (greedy routing), using the **expected permanent-upgrade levels** for that point in progression (from the Economy Simulator, §6.2).
2. It asserts: the expert bot reaches ≥ 1★ target ×1.10 (winnable with margin), 3★ is reachable, there are no unreachable pads or stations (pathfinding check), and no orders need products the level doesn't have.
3. Levels 1–10 are also run with a **Novice Bot** (50 % efficiency) and must still win.
4. Curve checks: chapter-average difficulty never decreases across chapters, the sawtooth is present, and bosses are in the right slots.
5. If any level fails, the generator nudges that level's target down in a seeded, deterministic way and the test is re-run. The final build ships with **0 failing levels**.

## 6. Meta game

### 6.1 Currencies
| Currency | Icon | Earned from | Spent on |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cash** (in level) | gold coin `$` | customers | pads during the level (resets every level) |

### 6.2 Economy
- **Level reward:** `R(n) = round(50 + 12·n^0.8) × starMult` (1★ ×1.0, 2★ ×1.25, 3★ ×1.6). Bosses ×2.
  *Examples: L1 = 62, L10 = 126, L100 = 528, L1000 = 3,064, L10000 = 19,070.*
- **Permanent upgrades (Kitchen tab):** 8 tracks, up to level 40 each. Cost is `base · 1.20^k`.
  Chef Speed (+2 %/lv) · Carry Capacity (+1 per 2 lv, 4→24) · Cook Speed (+2.5 %) · Patience (+1.5 %) · Tip Bonus (+3 %) · Starting Cash · Staff Speed (+2 %) · Store Income (+4 %).
- **Economy Simulator** (unit test): plays a "typical player" (average 2★, buys the cheapest useful upgrade) across 10,000 levels. It checks pacing: a Store becomes affordable roughly when planned, there are no dead zones with nothing to buy, and the expected upgrade levels feed the Validator.

### 6.4 Style: Themes, Skins, Pets ("theme option")
- **Chef Skins:** 10 species × outfits (apron colours, hats: toque, cap, bandana, crown, astronaut helmet…). Prices run from 2k Bucks to 500 Gems.
- **Pets** (follow you; each has one small ability): Pug (coin magnet radius), Parrot (announces troubles early), Hamster (+1 carry), Kitten (staff +5 % speed), Robo-Bot (auto-collects small piles).
- **Interior Themes** (decor packs that work in any Store: tables, chairs, floor tint, wall trims, lamps): Classic, Pastel Pop, Neon Night, Cozy Cabin, Royal Gold, Spooky, Winter Festive, Tropical.

### 6.5 Daily and engagement systems
- **Login calendar:** 7-day loop; day 7 gives a chest + gems.
- **Daily Spin:** 1 free per day.
- **Daily Missions:** 3 per day (for example "Serve 40 fries", "Get a ×3 combo"). Finishing all 3 opens a chest.
- **Recipe Book:** 60 collectible cards (recipes, customers, stores). Completing a set gives permanent tiny bonuses.
- **Achievements:** 50, with gem rewards.
- **Chef Rank:** XP from serving. Rank-ups give rewards.
- **Offline / Store income:** a "Welcome back" popup shows what your Stores earned.

### 6.6 Level lock rules (as you asked)
- **Debug and release:** on first launch **only level 1 is unlocked**. Finishing level N with ≥ 1★ unlocks N+1.
- **Debug only** (`BuildConfig.DEBUG_AD_UNLOCK = true`): tapping **any locked level** opens a game-styled "Unlock Level N?" dialog with a **Watch Ad** button. Tapping it plays a 3-second **mock ad** placeholder, then that level is unlocked (saved, with a confetti burst).
- **Release:** tapping a locked level shakes the node and shows "Finish Level X to unlock". There is no ad UI at all.
- All ad calls go through one `AdsProvider` interface (`DebugMockAds` / `NoAds`), so real ads can be added later in one place.

## 8. Graphics engine and asset pipeline

### 8.1 Why native Kotlin + a custom GLES renderer (not Unity, Filament or Godot)
- Your project is already Kotlin/Compose, and your test phone is a **Unisoc UMS9230E with a Mali-G57 MP1, 720×1600, 60/90 Hz, Android 16**, which is a low-end GPU. A lean, purpose-built toon renderer gives **full control of the look and of performance**.
- It needs no editor licences or sign-ins, no large engine downloads, and no native libraries. The APK target is **< 60 MB** (the reference is about 231 MB).
- Unity is the alternative: it's the same engine as the reference, but it needs your Unity sign-in, a multi-GB editor download, and slower build iteration. I don't recommend it here.

### 8.2 "Grill Engine" features (`:engine` module)
- GLSurfaceView with GLES 3.0. EGL config RGBA8888 + depth24 + **4× MSAA** (nearly free on Mali tile-based GPUs).
- Fixed-step 60 Hz simulation with render interpolation. Zero allocations per frame (pools).
- **Toon shading:** smooth 3-band ramp + **baked ambient occlusion in vertex colours** + rim light + hemisphere ambient + soft **PCF shadow map** (1024²) that follows the camera.
- **Inverted-hull outlines** on characters and hero props, in cocoa colour.
- **Day→night lighting** per shift (sun angle and colour, ambient, emissive lamps, additive light-pool decals).
- **GPU instancing** for food stacks, coins, chairs, foliage and character parts, keeping draw calls under 120.
- **Characters** are rigid-part rigs (head, body, arms, legs, ears, tail, hat slot) with **procedural animation**: walk, run, idle breathing, carry pose, sit, eat-chomp, happy jump, angry stomp, sleep, cheer, plus blinking eyes. Spring "jiggle" on ears and tails.
- **Stack physics:** a per-item spring offset makes carried stacks sway naturally.
- **Particles:** confetti, sparkles, grill steam, smoke, fire, coin bursts, dust puffs on footsteps, ketchup splats, stars, snow/rain (Store weather).
- **World-space UI:** order bubbles, price tags, progress rings and emotes, rendered crisply from a dynamic text atlas drawn with the game font.
- **Camera:** smooth damped follow, look-ahead, unlock pans, punch-zoom, small shakes.
- **Navigation:** grid A* + queue slots + local separation for NPCs.
- **Quality tiers** (auto-detected): Low (blob shadows, no MSAA, 30 fps) / **Medium (your Realme: shadows + MSAA 4×, 60 fps)** / High (+ bloom pass, 90/120 fps, 2048² shadows). Dynamic resolution kicks in if frame time goes over 18 ms.

### 8.3 Asset pipeline (`tools/`, all generated by us)
